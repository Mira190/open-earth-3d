# Blue Hour: the story

Seven evenings at the Shibuya Scramble, 10 to 16 October. One camera with seven frames left in it.
One question that turns over every evening: **why did he not cross the street?**

This is the story bible: the premise and its rules, what was learned from the works it stands on,
the truth of what happened in 1964, the seven episodes beat by beat, the clues and where each is
planted, and what the game has to be able to do to tell it. `cities/shibuya/city.json` `game.story`
holds the episodes as data (`web/game/story.js` plays them); episode 1 is built, the rest are
written here first.

The people and the crime are invented. The place, the dates, the hours and the city's history are
real, and the story is built so that the real things are the clues.

## 1. The premise in one breath

On 10 October, at 17:14, as the blue hour begins, the crossing fills and the world stops. Only you
still move, and a young woman at the kerb dressed for 1964. She has waited on that corner at that
hour for sixty years for a man who never came. She gives you his camera. There are seven frames
left on a roll he began in 1964, and the twenty-nine frames before them were never developed.

Every photograph you take in the stopped world, from the right place, brings up the picture that
was taken from that same place in 1964, laid over yours. Seven frames, seven evenings, seven
pictures out of the past. The first one shows the woman herself, waiting at the corner, taken from
across the street on the evening she says she stood alone.

He came. He photographed her. He did not cross. The story is finding out why, and then doing
something about it with the last frame.

## 2. The rules of the Stop

A mystery is only fair if its impossible thing has rules, said early and never broken.

1. **The Stop** comes once an evening, at sunset on the real sun's clock (17:14 on the 10th, a
   minute or so earlier each evening after), and lasts nine minutes. It comes when the crossing is
   full.
2. In the Stop the world has no time: people, traffic, trains, rain and screens hold. Whoever
   carries the camera moves.
3. **An afterimage** also moves. An afterimage is not a ghost. It is what a person left behind at a
   place and an hour they never walked on from: a promise unkept, a sentence unsaid. The living can
   leave one. (Episode 4 proves it; episode 6 depends on it.)
4. An afterimage stays within a few steps of where it was left. It cannot see past its own kerb,
   and it cannot see a photograph. It can only say what it knew at that hour, and it can be wrong.
5. **The camera** keeps the tenth of October open for as long as there is film in it. A frame taken
   from where a frame of 1964 was taken, facing as it faced, prints as both (the double exposure).
   A frame taken anywhere else is only a photograph, and it is gone all the same.
6. Seven frames. There is no eighth. A frame wasted is a clue lost, and the story goes on without
   it (section 8, "Failing").

## 3. What the best of them do, and what is taken from each

The brief was a plot that pulls like a short serial drama and satisfies like a detective novel.
These are the works studied, what makes each one work, and what Blue Hour adopts. Nothing of their
look, characters or text is used.

| Work | What makes it work | What Blue Hour takes |
|---|---|---|
| *Return of the Obra Dinn* (2018) | Deduction from frozen moments; the game never tells you that you are right until several answers agree | The Stop is a frozen moment you read for evidence. A photograph is how you commit to an answer, and the double exposure is the confirmation |
| *Her Story* (2015), *Immortality* (2022) | The player assembles the truth out of order; testimony that is sincere and still wrong | Each afterimage tells only what they knew at their hour. Nobody lies outright except one; the truth is in where the accounts do not fit |
| *Outer Wilds* (2019) | A short fixed span, repeated; the only thing you carry forward is what you know | Nine minutes an evening. Nothing is levelled up; what you keep is the contact sheet |
| *Ace Attorney* (2001–) | One contradiction per testimony, broken by presenting one piece of evidence; a reversal every case | Each episode has one statement to break with one print. Each ends with the case turned over |
| *Ghost Trick* (2010) | A last reveal about who someone is that makes every earlier scene mean something else, with every clue shown in plain sight | Sumiko's reveal in episode 6, planted in the first shot of episode 1 |
| *Disco Elysium* (2019) | The detective's own reason for being there is part of the case | Who hired you, and why you were told to be at that kerb at 17:14 |
| *428: Shibuya Scramble* (2008) | Shibuya itself as the board; hour stamps; "to be continued" as a weapon | Real hours on screen, the district as the map of the case, a hard cut on a question every evening |
| *Life is Strange* (2015) | A photograph as the hinge of time; an ending that is one act, not a boss | The last frame is one photograph, and taking it is the ending |
| *Paranormasight* (2023), *AI: The Somnium Files* (2019) | Local history turned into the engine of the mystery | 1964 Shibuya is not set dressing: each real fact is a clue (section 4) |
| K. Higashino, *The Devotion of Suspect X* (2005) | A howdunit whose last turn is not who did it but what someone gave up, and for whom | Two people each took the other's guilt on themselves and kept silent for sixty years |
| A. Christie; the Knox and Van Dine rules | Fair play: every clue before the reader, the answer the least expected of the people already met | The table in section 7: every answer is shown at least an episode before it is needed |
| Short serial dramas (vertical dramas, 2022–) | A hook in the first thirty seconds, one reversal per episode, an ending on the worst possible second, a secret identity paid off late, feeling stated plainly | The shape of every episode (section 6): hook, one task, one reversal, cut. No episode over twelve minutes |
| M. Shinkai, *Your Name* (2016) | The hour of twilight as the only time two worlds touch | The blue hour as the door, and rain kept for the one evening it means something |

Three rules come out of this and govern every episode:

- **One question per episode, answered, and a worse one asked in the same breath.**
- **The player proves it; nobody explains it.** A reveal arrives as a print in the player's hand
  before any character says it aloud.
- **The city is the witness.** The sun's angle, a sight line, a building that was not there yet:
  the evidence is geometry the map really has, so a player who looks can get ahead of the story.

## 4. The real Shibuya the story is made of

| The Hachiko statue was moved and turned in 1989 when the plaza was rebuilt | Why the standpoint of the first 1964 frame is hard to find: the landmark in it has moved (episode 2's puzzle) |

| The Shuto Expressway's Route 3 over Shibuya opened that same month | The deadline everyone on the works was under |
| The Tamaden tram ran from Shibuya to Sangenjaya until 1969 | How close fifteen minutes away is, and a tram stop where trams no longer come |

Before a line of an episode is written into the data, its facts are checked again against the
sources in `docs/cities/shibuya.md` and a dated photograph where one exists; a fact that does not
hold is cut from the plot, not bent.

## 5. The truth (what the player assembles)

**The people of 1964.**

- **Sumiko Hayase** 早瀬澄子, 22. Runs a lift at the Toyoko department store. The afterimage at the
  Hachiko kerb.
- **Akira Mizuno** 水野晶, 26. Lecturer at the planetarium. He photographs the night sky over
  Shibuya from the dome's roof, month by month, to record the stars the city's lights are putting
  out. The camera is his. He is the man who is not in the photograph, because he took them all.
- **Tatsuo Hayase** 早瀬辰雄, 31. Sumiko's brother. A foreman on the river works.
- **Soichi Kanbara** 神原宗一, 38. A section chief on the works, and the man the family means
  Sumiko to marry. He dies on the planetarium's roof at about 15:20 on 10 October.
- **The boy**, 14. Shines shoes by the station. He is given a hundred yen to carry a camera across
  the street.

**What happened.** In September a labourer died in the river culvert in the rush to finish before
the Games. Kanbara kept it out of the record and Tatsuo, who had been in charge that night, was
paid to stay quiet. Akira, photographing the sky from the roof, had the works in the bottom of his
frames night after night, and on one of them the night itself. He did not know what he had until
Kanbara came asking for the film.

Akira and Sumiko had decided to leave Tokyo on the night train on the 10th. They would meet at the
Hachiko corner at sunset, "when the sky is the colour of the dome before the show".

On the afternoon of the 10th, with the city indoors, Kanbara went up to the roof for the film.
Sumiko, on her break, had gone up to warn Akira and met Kanbara instead. He took her by the wrist;
she tore free, leaving her white glove in his hand, shoved him and ran. He was on his feet when she
left. Ten minutes later Tatsuo came up. Kanbara, frightened now, told him the culvert would be put
on the foreman. Tatsuo pushed him. Akira, coming up the stair with the rings being drawn in the sky,
took one frame of two men at the parapet without understanding it, and then found Kanbara below
with Sumiko's glove.

**The double sacrifice.** Akira believed Sumiko had done it. He walked into the police box at the
station and said that he had. On the way to the car he asked the detective for one minute at the
kerb opposite the Hachiko corner. He took one photograph of her waiting, with himself and the two
officers in the shop window beside him, gave the camera and a hundred yen to a shoeshine boy, and
said: *take it across. Tell her to open it.* He did not cross, because to be seen with her was to
make her his reason.

The boy carried the camera over, saw a policeman watching, pushed it into her hands and ran without
a word.

Sumiko heard the next day that Akira had confessed to killing Kanbara. She believed he had done it,
for her, because of what she had told him about Kanbara. She kept silent so that it would not have
been for nothing. Akira had told her once never to open the back of a camera: light kills what is
inside. She never opened it.

So each of them believed the other guilty, and each kept the secret to protect the other.

**After.** Akira served twelve years. He came out in 1976 under his mother's family name and took
a room in Sangenjaya, fifteen minutes from the corner. Every 10 October he stood at the kerb where
he had taken the photograph, and did not cross: she had a life, he thought, and he was the worst
thing that had happened in it. Tatsuo told Sumiko the truth on his deathbed in 1989. She looked for
a Mizuno and found nobody. She went on coming to her kerb every 10 October. For forty-eight years
they stood on opposite kerbs of the same crossing for one hour a year and never saw each other
through it.

Akira died in March of this year, aged 86. His will paid for one thing: a survey photograph of the
Hachiko corner, taken from the opposite kerb, at 17:14 on 10 October.

**You** are the survey photographer who took the job. You arrived at 17:15. You were on the
crossing, not at the kerb, when it stopped.

**Sumiko is alive.** She is 82. She is the old woman with the clear umbrella who is at the Hachiko
kerb in the opening shot of every episode. The girl of 22 is what she left there.

This is the first 10 October on which both of them have an afterimage at the crossing. That is why
it stops.

## 6. The seven episodes

Each episode is one evening and one frame: a cold open (15 seconds), the street (a minute or two
with the living), the Stop (nine minutes: go to the afterimage, hear them, find the standpoint,
take the one photograph), the print, and a cut on the worst possible second. Ten to twelve minutes.

Episodes 1, 2, 6 and 7 are played inside the hundred metres round the spawn. The story is written
that way on purpose: the level that has to be finest is the one seen four times.

### Weather, light and sound, by what the evening is for

Weather is cast like a character: it is there because the episode needs it, never as a default.
Six evenings are dry. Rain comes once.

| Evening | Sky | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clear, the blue hour at its cleanest | The first sight of the stopped world must be legible and quiet: a held breath, not weather. The hush when the street's sound drops out is the effect |
| 2 | Clear | A puzzle of sight lines: nothing between the player and the far kerb |
| 3 | Clear and cold, the longest view | From the roof the whole case is laid out below, and the first stars are out where he used to count them |
| 4 | Overcast, mist in the lanes | The one evening away from the crossing, with a secret kept for sixty years: close, soft, no horizon |
| 5 | Clear, low sun before the Stop | The sun is the evidence; it has to be seen and its shadows have to be sharp |
| 6 | Clear, cloud building; the first drops on the cut | The weather turns as the case does |
| 7 | Rain | Every umbrella is up, so the one clear umbrella can be followed across the whole crossing; and rain held still in the air is the picture of the Stop that the story has saved for last |

Sound follows the same rule. The street is the score: the crossing's signal, footsteps, a train on
the viaduct. Rain, when it comes, is mixed under the voices as a soft, low wash, never a hiss, and
the Stop cuts it to silence. An evening opens quietly enough to hear one person say one line.

### 1. You came late · 10 October, clear · the Scramble (built)

- **Hook.** A clear evening, the sky still blue over the screens. The crossing fills, and stops. One
  person turns to look at you.
- **The Stop.** Sumiko at the Hachiko kerb: *You came late. Sixty years I have stood on this corner.
  I was not waiting for you.* She gives you the camera and asks to be shown what the crossing has
  become. You photograph the screens.
- **The print.** Tonight's screens, and under them, faintly, the same corner in 1964 with a young
  woman waiting at it: Sumiko herself. (To add to the built episode: the double exposure on the
  last card, and the old woman with the clear umbrella at the kerb in the opening shot.)
- **Reversal.** She said she stood alone. Somebody was across the street with a camera.
- **Cut.** *Six frames left. Who took this?*

### 2. The man who is not in the photograph · 11 October, clear · Center Gai and the far kerb

- **Hook.** Shun at the vintage shop turns the camera over. A name is scratched under the base
  plate: 水野. *There is a roll in it. Twenty-nine gone. Whatever is on it is still on it.*
- **The task.** Find where the 1964 frame was taken from. The picture has Hachiko in it, and
  Hachiko has moved since: the player has to line up what has not moved (the railway viaduct's
  piers, the slope of Dogenzaka) and ends up on the kerb under the big screen.
- **The Stop.** A boy of fourteen with a shoeshine box by the Center Gai arch. *A man gave me a
  hundred yen to carry a camera across. He could have carried it himself. It was thirty steps.*
  Press him with the print: *He had a policeman on each side of him.*
- **The print.** The far kerb in 1964: a shop window, and in the glass three men, the middle one
  with a camera raised. The man who is not in the photographs, in a photograph.
- **Reversal.** He came. He was under arrest.
- **Cut.** *What for?* The boy: *They said he had killed a man that afternoon. Up where the stars
  are.*

### 3. Where the stars are · 12 October, clear and cold · SHIBUYA SKY

- **Hook.** Yuna at the lift: *Forty-five floors in under a minute. My grandmother ran a lift in the
  old store. She said the job was the same: you take people up and you do not ask why.*
- **The task.** The planetarium's roof is gone; Hikarie stands on it. The only place it can be seen
  from now is above. On the roof at blue hour, with Taro's help on where to stand, photograph down
  on to Hikarie.
- **The Stop.** On the roof, a man in a good suit who does not know he is dead: Kanbara. *I had my
  back to the stair. I was watching the rings, like every fool in Tokyo. All I know is whose glove
  I was holding.*
- **The print.** The dome, the parapet, two figures at it, and in the sky behind them five rings
  half drawn: ten past three, to the minute.
- **Reversal.** The glove is a lift girl's. The evidence points at Sumiko.
- **Cut.** Back at the kerb, you ask her. *I told you I was not waiting for you. I was waiting for
  him, or for the police. Whichever came first.*

### 4. A hundred yen · 13 October, overcast, mist in the lanes · Sangenjaya

- **Hook.** The bath-house at Sangenjaya. Mr Tanaka, seventy-four, sees the camera and sits down.
  You met him two evenings ago. He was fourteen.
- **What it proves.** The boy is alive. An afterimage is not the dead (rule 3).
- **The living witness.** *He said: tell her to open it. I saw the policeman looking and I ran. One
  sentence. I have had it in my mouth for sixty years.* And then: *He did not die in prison. He
  came out in '76. He lived two lanes from here under another name. We shared this bath for forty
  years and I never told him either. He went in March.*
- **The task and the Stop.** In the triangle of lanes, find the standpoint of a frame taken at
  night: Tatsuo Hayase in his site helmet, taking an envelope from Kanbara. Tatsuo's afterimage is
  not here; the lane is empty and stopped. The photograph is the only witness.
- **Reversal.** Tanaka hands you a letter: the order for your survey. It is from Mizuno's estate.
  A dead man hired you.
- **Cut.** *He stood at the far kerb every tenth of October. I think he is standing there now.*

### 5. The river under the street · 14 October, clear, low sun · Shibuya Stream and the viaduct

- **Hook.** The river comes out of its pipe at Shibuya Stream. A plaque gives the year it went
  under. It does not give a name.
- **The Stop.** Tatsuo at the culvert's mouth, helmet under his arm. His account: he was on the
  site until four. He was nowhere near the roof.
- **The task.** Break it with the sun. The roof frame from episode 3 has a second shadow in it,
  falling towards the camera. At ten past three on 10 October the sun stands where the map says it
  stands: the player takes the bearing at the Stream and sets it against the print. The shadow is
  of a man in a site helmet. (For one breath it is the player's own silhouette: the surveyor's hard
  hat. A red herring the player clears themselves.)
- **The print.** The culvert works in September, at night, lamps and a covered shape on a
  stretcher: what Kanbara wanted the film for.
- **Reversal.** Tatsuo did it. Sumiko pushed a living man and ran; her brother came ten minutes
  later. *Mizuno chose it himself. He thought she had done it. I let him think it.*
- **Cut.** *She knows. I told her myself, in '89, the week I died. Ask her why she still stands
  there.*

### 6. The far kerb · 15 October, clear, cloud building · the Scramble

- **Hook.** One frame for tonight, one for tomorrow. Sumiko, before you speak: *You have been to
  see my brother.*
- **The task.** Frame 29, the last one he took: the photograph of her from the far kerb, the one
  the boy was sent across with. Stand where Akira stood. This is the frame episode 2 found the
  place for; now it is taken at his minute, 17:14, facing as he faced.
- **The print.** Sumiko at the corner, 1964, sharp this time. In the window beside the lens, Akira
  between two officers, his free hand raised to her. Not waving. Holding up the planetarium's
  ticket for the last show of the night: the train they were going to miss it for.
- **Reversal one.** You carry the print across to her. The afterimage cannot see it (rule 4). A
  hand comes past your shoulder and takes it: the old woman with the clear umbrella, who has been
  at this kerb in every opening shot. *You came late*, she says, as she said to you on the first
  evening, when you thought it was the girl. Sumiko Hayase, eighty-two.
- **Reversal two.** She looked for him after 1989 and found no Mizuno. *He is over there, isn't he.
  Every year I have felt the shutter. I have never once seen him.*
- **Cut.** One frame. Two kerbs. The lights change, and the first drops fall.

### 7. Scramble · 16 October, rain · the middle of the crossing

- **Hook.** Rain, for the first time in the story. 17:06. Every umbrella in Shibuya is up; the
  crossing fills from every side and stops, and the rain stops in the air with it.
- **The Stop.** Two afterimages, one on each kerb: the girl of 1964 and, opposite, an old man in a
  raincoat with his hands empty, new this year. Neither can see past their own kerb. In 1964 there
  was no way to cross this junction corner to corner. There is now.
- **The task.** Stand in the middle of the scramble, where the diagonals meet, and take the one
  photograph that has both kerbs in it. Both of them, in one frame. The counter goes to 0.
- **What happens.** The print is the first picture he has ever been in with her. The Stop does not
  end on its clock: every pedestrian light goes green at once, and in the held world the two of
  them step off their kerbs and walk the diagonal and meet where you are standing, and go out, like
  a breath let go.
- **The living.** The world runs. An old woman with a clear umbrella crosses the scramble corner to
  corner, slowly, in the rain, through everyone, to the far kerb. She stands where he stood, and
  looks back at where she stood.
- **Last card.** The print, and under it the two dates. *Blue Hour.*
- **After the card.** The counter, at 0, turns over to S. Somebody has loaded a new roll.

## 8. What the game has to do to tell it

In the order it should be built. Each is small; none is a new world.

1. **The double exposure.** A print is the frame taken, with the episode's 1964 plate laid over it
   when the standpoint and the aim are right (`task.stand`, `task.within_m` beside the existing
   `task.subject`, `task.within_deg`; `task.plate`). Seven plates, drawn for the story in one hand,
   monochrome, from dated photographs of the same views. This is the reward of every episode and
   wants the most care.
2. **The contact sheet.** The one piece of standing interface: seven frames in a strip, filled as
   they are taken, each opening to its print and one line. It replaces the map, the passport and
   the quest card.
3. **Finding a standpoint.** The beacon gives way to the print itself: hold the 1964 plate up
   against the street (a ghosted overlay in the viewfinder) and walk until the piers line up.
   Episode 2 teaches it; 3, 5 and 6 use it.
4. **Pressing a statement.** In an afterimage's lines, one line can be answered with a print
   (`press: { with: <frame>, lines: [...] }`): the existing dialogue with a choice of prints.
5. **The living.** A minute with a present-day person before the Stop (the existing people and
   dialogue: Shun, Yuna, Taro, Mr Tanaka), and the old woman with the clear umbrella placed at the
   kerb in every episode's opening.
6. **Episodes and a save.** An episode menu on the title (the contact sheet again), the frames
   kept, the next evening unlocked by the last.
7. **The sun as evidence** (episode 5): the bearing and height of the sun at a given minute, read
   off in the viewfinder. The map already has the real sun.
8. **The ending** (episode 7): two afterimages, both in frame; all the walk lights green in the
   Stop; two figures walking a path; one living figure crossing afterwards.

**Failing.** A frame taken from the wrong place is spent: the print is only tonight. The episode
still ends, on a card that says what the frame did not show, and the evening can be played again.
The Stop running out does the same. Nobody dies and nothing is lost for good except the minute.

**Places.** The Scramble's hero level carries 1, 2, 6 and 7. SHIBUYA SKY's roof and lift (3) and
Sangenjaya's lanes and bath-house (4) are built. Shibuya Stream (5) needs a look at what stands
there now before the episode is written to it; if it does not hold up, the episode moves to the
railway viaduct south of the station, where the river's line can still be read.

## 9. The voice

- Short lines. Nobody explains the rules; they say what they saw.
- Sumiko's first words of each evening are in Japanese, then English, as built.
- Every episode's last line is a question or a fact that reopens the case. Never a summary.
- No character speaks of data, surveys of buildings or how the map was made
  (`tools/voice_check.py`). The player's trade is photographing streets; that is all that is said
  of it.
