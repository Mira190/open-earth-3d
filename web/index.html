      How a city is built

      1. Footprints and heights from Overture Maps (OpenStreetMap and Microsoft ML), each height tagged with where it came from.
      2. Bare-earth terrain and aerial imagery from the national or state open-data service, checked against an independent source.
      3. Missing heights estimated from neighbours and calibrated against published heights, with a measured error for every source.
      4. The real sun from the NOAA equations in the city's own time zone, ray-cast through a 2 m surface model.

      One config file per city: a bounding box, the data services, its public places and a tour. The same files feed the web view, the analyses and a Cycles render in Blender.

      Stops through the model. Drag the view at any time to take over; the tour waits for you.

        Date and time

        Local clock time.

        Hours of direct sun

        Ground-level sun hours across the city centre for the chosen date, every 20 minutes from sunrise to sunset, ray-cast through the 2 m surface model.

        Method

        Sun position from the NOAA solar equations (about 0.01° accuracy). Shadows in the 3D view use a shadow map; the numbers in this panel and in Sun access come from ray casting, which does not depend on screen resolution.
